## What it checks

Once a day, queries the **domain registry** for a domain and records the registrar, the
registration expiry date, the days remaining and the nameservers on file.

The check fails when days remaining is **below** your warning threshold, or when the registry
returns no usable expiry date.

## Fields

| Field | Range | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | bare registrable domain | — |
| Warn below | 1 – 365 days | 30 days |
| Interval | 20 s – 24 h | 24 h |
| Failures before down | 1 – 10 | 2 |
| Re-alert every | 5 min – 24 h, or off | off |

Use the **registrable** domain — `example.com`, not `www.example.com`. Registration exists at
the registrable level; a subdomain has no registry record of its own.

## It does not require the domain to resolve

This is a registry lookup, not a DNS lookup, so a domain with no A record is monitorable.

That matters more than it first appears. A domain used mainly for email often publishes only MX
and TXT records at the apex and nothing else — and a monitoring tool that resolves the domain
before checking it would refuse to watch exactly the domains most likely to lapse unnoticed.

## Choosing a threshold

| Situation | Suggested |
|---|---|
| Auto-renew on, card known good | 30 days |
| Auto-renew on, card owned by someone who left | 60 days |
| Manual renewal, or a registrar you rarely sign into | 90 days |

For anything that matters, err high. The renewal itself takes two minutes; finding out who
controls the registrar account can take a week.

## Failure messages

```
🔴 DOWN — example-com-registration (example.com): domain registration expires in 21 days
🔴 DOWN — example-com-registration (example.com): registry returned no expiry date
🔴 DOWN — example-com-registration (example.com): could not parse registry expiry date
```

On the monitor:

```
BigRock Solutions Ltd. · expires 2027-05-29T18:41:10 · 297 days remaining
```

## Registry coverage

Most gTLDs — `.com`, `.net`, `.org`, `.io`, `.dev`, `.app` — publish a machine-readable expiry
date and work well.

Some ccTLDs publish little or nothing; the registry may withhold the expiry date entirely. When
that happens the monitor reports `registry returned no expiry date` rather than guessing.

If your TLD is not supported, this monitor cannot help, and a calendar reminder is the honest
alternative.

## Nameservers

The nameservers on file at the registry are recorded alongside the expiry. A registrar-level
hijack — where nameservers change without your involvement — shows up here, which is a different
signal from a [DNS monitor](/docs/monitors/dns) watching records inside the zone.

## Worked example

```
Domain:               example.com
Warn below:           60 days
Interval:             24 hours
Failures before down: 1
Re-alert every:       24 hours
```

<Callout type="note">
If you set up one monitor in this product, make it this one. Thirty seconds, once, against a
failure that takes down every service on the domain simultaneously — including the email that
would have warned you.
</Callout>
